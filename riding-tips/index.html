Highway Riding Tips

Essential safety tips and gear recommendations for long-distance rides.

Back to Home

Before You Ride

Long highway rides are one of the best parts of motorcycle touring in the Philippines. But proper preparation makes the difference between an amazing trip and a dangerous one.

  • Check your motorcycle — tire pressure, brakes, chain tension, oil level, and lights. Do this every time before a long ride.
  • Check the weather — Philippine weather changes fast. Avoid riding during typhoon season (June–November) if possible.
  • Plan your route — know where the gas stations, rest stops, and emergency services are along the way.
  • Tell someone your plans — share your route and ETA with a friend or family member.

💡 Pro Tip: Fill up your tank every 80–100km. Gas stations can be far apart in rural areas, especially in the Cordilleras and Bicol.

Essential Gear for Highway Rides

Having the right gear can save your life. Here's what every rider needs for long-distance highway riding:

  1. Full-face helmet — DOT-certified minimum. Protects your head and face from debris, wind, and in case of accidents.
  2. Riding jacket with armor — mesh for tropical climate, with elbow and shoulder protection.
  3. Gloves — protect your hands from sun, wind, and road rash. Even cheap gloves are better than none.
  4. Riding boots or sturdy shoes — ankle support is critical, especially on uneven roads.
  5. Rain gear — a lightweight poncho or rain jacket. Philippine rain comes fast and hard.

On the Road

Once you're riding, stay alert and follow these guidelines:

  • Ride defensively — assume other drivers don't see you. Stay visible, use your horn, and keep distance.
  • Take breaks every 2 hours — fatigue is a killer. Stop, stretch, hydrate, and eat.
  • Avoid night riding — many provincial roads have no streetlights, and stray animals are common.
  • Watch for road hazards — potholes, gravel, water puddles (you don't know how deep), and animal crossings.
  • Use your mirrors constantly — check behind you before changing lanes or overtaking.

⚠️ Safety Reminder: The Philippine highway speed limit is 60–80 kph for motorcycles. OverSpeeding is the #1 cause of motorcycle accidents on provincial roads.

Staying Comfortable

Long rides can be exhausting in the Philippine heat. Here's how to stay comfortable:

  • Wear breathable, moisture-wicking clothing under your riding gear
  • Bring a hydration pack or water bottle — dehydration happens fast
  • Use sunglasses or a visor insert to reduce glare
  • Apply sunscreen on exposed skin — you'll get burned even on cloudy days
  • Carry snacks — energy bars, dried fruit, or local kakanin

Emergency Kit

Always carry these essentials in your top box or bag:

  • Tire repair kit (tubeless plug kit or patch kit)
  • Basic tool set (wrenches, screwdrivers, zip ties)
  • First aid kit (bandages, alcohol, pain relievers)
  • Power bank for your phone
  • Emergency contact numbers written down (not just in your phone)

Highway riding in the Philippines is an incredible experience. The scenery, the food stops, the feeling of freedom — it's all worth the preparation. Ride safe, ride smart, and enjoy the journey.
