The Ultimate Road Trip Checklist

Planning a motorcycle road trip in the Philippines? Here's everything you need to prepare — from gear to documents to mindset. This guide covers both overnight rides and multi-day adventures.

Documents to Bring

  • Driver's license — make sure it's valid and has the motorcycle restriction code
  • OR/CR of your motorcycle — original or certified copy. Traffic enforcers will ask for this.
  • Valid ID — secondary ID in case of emergencies
  • Cash — many provincial areas don't accept cards. Bring enough for gas, food, toll, and emergencies.
  • Emergency contacts — written on paper, not just saved in your phone

💡 Budget Tip: For a 3-day ride, prepare at least ₱3,000–5,000 per day for gas, food, and accommodation. Provincial lodging is cheap (₱500–1,000/night).

Motorcycle Preparation

Your bike is your lifeline on the road. Do a thorough check before any long trip:

  1. Tires — check pressure and tread depth. Bring a tire repair kit for tubeless tires.
  2. Brakes — test front and rear brakes. Check brake fluid level.
  3. Chain — clean, lubricate, and adjust tension if needed.
  4. Oil — check level and color. Change if it's due.
  5. Lights — headlight, tail light, brake light, turn signals. All must work.
  6. Horn — test it. You'll use it a lot in the Philippines.

Packing List

Pack light but smart. Here's what goes in my top box and bag:

  • Riding gear — helmet, jacket, gloves, rain poncho
  • Tools — basic wrench set, tire repair kit, zip ties, electrical tape, duct tape
  • Electronics — phone mount, power bank, charging cables
  • Personal items — toiletries, sunscreen, alcohol wipes, towel
  • Clothes — 2 sets of riding clothes, 1 set of casual clothes, jacket for cold areas (Baguio, Sagada, Banaue)
  • First aid — bandages, Betadine, paracetamol, Biogesic, oral rehydration salts

⚠️ Weather Warning: Always bring a rain poncho even if the forecast says sunny. Philippine weather is unpredictable, especially in the mountains.

On the Road Tips

  • Start early — leave by 5-6 AM to avoid traffic and maximize daylight
  • Fill up often — don't let your tank go below half, especially in remote areas
  • Use Waze or Google Maps — download offline maps for areas with poor signal
  • Take photos at viewpoints — but don't stop in dangerous spots
  • Eat local — try the carinderia (local eatery) food. It's cheap, authentic, and delicious.
  • Respect the locals — smile, say thank you, and be polite. Filipinos are warm and helpful.

After the Ride

When you get home, don't forget to:

  • Clean and wash your motorcycle
  • Check for any damage or loose parts
  • Lube the chain
  • Back up your photos
  • Share your experience on RidePinas!

Road trips are what motorcycle riding is all about. The Philippines has some of the most beautiful roads in Southeast Asia — from coastal highways to mountain passes. Plan well, ride safe, and enjoy every kilometer.
